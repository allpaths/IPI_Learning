The Supplementary Material holds everything a careful reader or referee would want to check but that would interrupt the paper: the full list of inputs and their status (S1), the Geant4 model and its limits (S2), the escape calculation (S3), the positronium model (S4), the cavity temperature (S5), beam heating (S6), the noise model and the \(T^3\) law (S7), and optics (S8).

Section by section#

S1. Model inputs (Table S1)#

Every number feeding Table II, tagged C (computed), G (Geant4), L (literature) or A (assumed). Of 20 rows, 13 are assumptions or design choices. That is normal for a design study, but it is why the paper says "under the stated assumptions".

S2. The Geant4 model and its limits#

Supplement · S2, paragraph 1… In this version and list, positrons that annihilate in flight are handled by a model that includes three-photon annihilation, and positrons at rest annihilate into two photons; ortho-positronium decay into three photons is available as an option but requires a user-supplied positronium fraction for each material [9]. We did not enable it, so the archived runs contain no positronium decays.

This is the key fact about the Geant4 model and positronium. Ref. [9] (Allison et al. 2027, by the Geant4 developers) describes the annihilation models in the Geant4 version used, 11.3.2 with the option4 list. That list uses a two-photon model at rest. Three-photon ortho-positronium decay exists, but Geant4 cannot work out how much positronium forms; the user has to set that fraction for each material. The archived code never set it. So the runs contain no positronium at all, and the 15% positronium fraction used in the main text has no Geant4 basis. It comes from the S4 model and the surface-physics literature.

Supplement · S2, the four resultsWe use four results from the model: … stopping depths … the fraction of infrared photons leaving the film that reach the collector's exit, 0.984 … the fraction of 1 keV positrons back-scattered from the film, 9% … 2.10 entries of particles above 400 keV into the scintillator shell per annihilation. The number exceeds two because a gamma photon that scatters back out of the shell can re-enter it and is counted again; it is not a detection efficiency. … Four limitations apply.

The limitations are the important part: (1) Geant4's low-energy transport isn't reliable at 100 eV; (2) back-scatter was simulated at 1 keV, not 100 eV, so the 10% used is an assumption; (3) because signal and background were injected, the model's infrared output tests neither; (4) the model forms no positronium, so it does not test the three-photon monitor and gives no basis for the 15% fraction. A re-run with Geant4's ortho-positronium models, with the fraction from S4, would give the monitor's efficiency.

S3. Infrared escape#

Transfer matrix with Ordal's Drude parameters for Al (plasma frequency 119 000 cm⁻¹, damping 660 cm⁻¹), angle-averaged. Key numbers: 0.104 at 3 nm, 0.0675 at 5 nm, 0.036 at 10 nm; thick-film floor 0.6–1%. Silicon backing changes 5 nm escape from 0.0675 to 0.0677.

S4. Positron stopping and positronium#

Makhov stopping profile, 150 nm diffusion length, and a positronium fraction of 0.15 + thermally activated part (0.25 eV), normalized to 0.5 at 300 K. A sanity check reproduces the ~55% surface return at 3 keV (model: 58%). The positronium parameters are assumptions, and none of them comes from Geant4, which forms no positronium (S2). The three-gamma rate measures them in place.

S5. Cavity temperature#

The wall/film ratio of in-band emission, assuming wall emissivity 0.01 and radius 4 cm: 6×10⁻⁸ (4 K), 0.011 (10 K), 0.64 (20 K) in the 77 K band. Hence 4–10 K, not 20 K.

S6. Beam heating and the substrate#

Requirement: the chopped change in thermal emission must be below 10% of the chopped signal. Tolerable temperature flicker: 2.4×10⁻⁸ K (77 K), 6.2×10⁻⁹ K (150 K). A bare 5 nm film would flicker by ~2×10⁻⁴ K; on 0.5 mm of silicon, ~1.1×10⁻⁹ K (margins 21 and 5.6). The ratio is independent of \(R\), since heating and signal both scale with the beam.

S7. Noise model and the \(T^3\) law#

Supplement · S7, Eq. (S2)\(P_\mathrm{th} = \eta f_\mathrm{coll}\,2\mathcal{A}\varepsilon\,\frac{2\pi k_b^4T^4}{h^3c^2}\int_{x_0(1-b/2)}^{x_0(1+b/2)}\frac{x^3dx}{e^x-1} \propto T^4\)

Substituting \(x = h\nu/k_bT\) shows the band integral doesn't depend on \(T\) (the band moves with \(T\)), leaving \(T^4\). Then NEP ∝ \(T^{5/2}\), signal ∝ \(T\), time ∝ \(T^3\). The script checks each ratio:

# scripts/explainer_build/cells/c08_scaling.py # The T^3 law and the lock-in factor (Supplement Sec. S7) import math, sys; sys.path.insert(0, "../../simulation") import sensitivity as S b77, b150 = S.budget(77.0), S.budget(150.0) print(f"P_th 150/77 = {b150['P_th_W']/b77['P_th_W']:.2f} (150/77)^4 = {(150/77)**4:.2f}") print(f"NEP 150/77 = {b150['NEP']/b77['NEP']:.2f} (150/77)^2.5 = {(150/77)**2.5:.2f}") print(f"tau 150/77 = {S.tau(1e8, b150)/S.tau(1e8, b77):.2f} (150/77)^3 = {(150/77)**3:.2f}") print(f"sine-wave lock-in kappa = sqrt(2)/pi = {math.sqrt(2)/math.pi:.3f}; tau longer by {(0.5/(math.sqrt(2)/math.pi))**2:.2f}x")
P_th 150/77 = 14.40 (150/77)^4 = 14.40 NEP 150/77 = 5.30 (150/77)^2.5 = 5.30 tau 150/77 = 7.39 (150/77)^3 = 7.39 sine-wave lock-in kappa = sqrt(2)/pi = 0.450; tau longer by 1.23x

S8. Optics#

Not designed in detail; η = 0.5 is assumed. Three requirements: strong out-of-band rejection (the film's total glow is far larger than its in-band glow), cold filters and light pipe, and a detector covering 92–179 µm plus the control band.

Questions a referee might ask#

Q1. Why should I trust Geant4 numbers you couldn't re-run?

Honest answer: the two that matter are easy to sanity-check: the collected fraction 0.984 equals the geometric coverage 0.983, and the stopping depth only sets the film thickness, which has a factor-of-three margin. The code is archived and can be re-run; a dedicated low-energy implantation calculation would be better at 100 eV. The positronium numbers do not depend on Geant4 at all (see Q3).

Q2. Your heating margin at 150 K is only 5.6. Isn't that thin?

Honest answer: it uses silicon's room-temperature conductivity, which is several times lower than its value at 77–150 K, and a steady-state (worst-case) response. The real margin should be larger, and the control band monitors any residual heating directly.

Q3. Each annihilation makes two gamma photons. How can the shell record 2.10 per annihilation?

Honest answer: because 2.10 counts entries, not photons. The detector code records a hit every time a particle crosses into the scintillator shell, and the count keeps every hit above 400 keV. It does not check whether the same photon was already counted. A 511 keV photon can cross the shell, or scatter in it, come back out of the inner surface, cross the empty cavity and enter the shell again on the far side. It can also bounce off the nitrogen shield outside the shell and come back. Each time it enters, it is counted again. So the number is "entries per annihilation", and the paper says so.

It is tempting to explain the extra 0.10 as three-photon decays of ortho-positronium. That cannot be right, for two reasons. First, the runs contain no positronium decays (S2). Second, a three-photon decay would make the count go down: it replaces two 511 keV photons with three photons sharing 1022 keV, and those are mostly below 400 keV. The main text does not use the 2.10 figure for any estimate, so no result changes. Counting unique tracks (one per photon) in a re-run would give the true detection efficiency, which can be at most 2.

Key takeaways#
