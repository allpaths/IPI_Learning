Where this section fits#

The Supplementary Material holds everything a careful reader or referee would want to check but that would interrupt the paper: the full list of inputs and their status (S1), the Geant4 model and its limits (S2), the escape calculation (S3), the positronium model (S4), the cavity temperature (S5), beam heating (S6), the noise model and the \(T^3\) law (S7), and optics (S8).

⚠ What was and wasn't re-runRe-run for this paper: ir_escape.py, ps_formation.py, sensitivity.py, background_estimates.py, make_figures.py. The Geant4 model was re-run with Geant4 11.4.2 (October 2026), with ortho-positronium switched on in a second set of runs and a scan of the ortho-positronium fraction from 0 to 0.675 in a third; the values in S2 come from those runs. Not run: analyze_response.py (needs the Geant4 scan files; sensitivity.py replaces it).

Section by section#

S1. Model inputs (Table S1)#

Every number feeding Table II, tagged C (computed), G (Geant4), L (literature) or A (assumed). Of 20 rows, 13 are assumptions or design choices. That is normal for a design study, but it is why the paper says "under the stated assumptions".

S2. The Geant4 model and its limits#

Supplement · S2, paragraph 1… It was first run with Geant4 11.3.2 and re-run with Geant4 11.4.2; the two agree within 2% for every quantity used here, and the values below are from the re-run. The custom process annihilates positrons in flight into two photons, which matters little, since about one positron in 10⁵ annihilates in flight. At rest, the physics list's default model gives two photons only, so the first runs contain no positronium decays. For the re-run we also enabled the ortho-positronium model of Geant4 11.3 and later, in which a stopped positron decays into three photons, with the Ore–Powell spectrum [9], with a probability set by the user for each material [10]. We set it in the film only; it is zero in every other material. The first runs used 0, 0.1125 and 0.15, from an earlier assumed positronium fraction of 15%. A second scan, also with Geant4 11.4.2, used 0, 0.1125, 0.25, 0.36, 0.5 and 0.675. The value 0.36 is the ortho-positronium share, three quarters, of the nominal positronium fraction of 0.48 …

This is the key fact about the Geant4 model and positronium. Ref. [10] (Allison et al. 2027, by the Geant4 developers) describes the annihilation models. The option4 list uses a two-photon model at rest. Three-photon ortho-positronium decay exists, but Geant4 cannot work out how much positronium forms; the user has to set that fraction for each material. The first runs never set it, so they contain no positronium at all. The re-run set it in the film only, first to 0, 0.1125 and 0.15 (from the old 15% positronium fraction), then, after the fraction was corrected to 0.48, to six values from 0 to 0.675. Ortho-positronium is three of the four spin states, so a positronium fraction of 0.48 means an ortho fraction of 0.36, and 0.9 means 0.675. The positronium fraction is still an input, not a Geant4 result. It now comes from measurements on clean Al(111) (S4).

One correction came with the re-run. An earlier version of this paragraph said that positrons annihilating in flight are handled by a model that includes three-photon annihilation. That is what option4 sets up, but the model's custom annihilation process (which adds the predicted photons) replaces the standard one and uses the two-photon model in flight. It makes no practical difference: only about one positron in 10⁵ annihilates in flight, and switching the three-photon in-flight model on changes nothing measurable.

Supplement · S2, the five resultsWe use five results from the model: … stopping depths 0.42, 0.81, 1.42, 2.73, 7.34 and 17.5 nm at 20 to 1000 eV, and 482 nm at 10 keV … the fraction of infrared photons leaving the film that reach the collector's exit, 0.984 … the fraction of positrons that leave the film without annihilating in it: 10% at 1 keV, and 6% for the 5 nm film at 100 eV … the gamma-ray response, counting each annihilation photon once: at least one of the two photons deposits more than 50 keV in 78% of annihilations, both in 28%; with a photopeak cut, 58% and 12% … the response to three-photon decays: \(R_\gamma\) is 0.035 with no positronium, 0.131 at f = 0.1125, 0.278 at 0.25, 0.424 at 0.36, 0.682 at 0.5 and 1.19 at 0.675 … It is not linear in f. The valley and photopeak counts per annihilation are each linear, V = 0.0249 + 0.524f and P = 0.707 − 0.574f, so \(R_\gamma = (a+bf)/(1+cf)\) … Inverted, \(f = (R_\gamma-a)/(b-cR_\gamma)\). … The first runs reported 2.10 particles above 400 keV entering the shell per annihilation. That count includes photoelectrons created inside the crystal (0.17 per annihilation); 1.93 distinct photons enter the shell above 400 keV per annihilation. Four limitations apply.

The limitations are the important part: (1) Geant4's low-energy transport isn't reliable at 100 eV; in the model half of the 100 eV positrons stop at exactly the same depth, which is a sign of that, and the 90th percentile (1.7 nm) marks that depth; (2) the main text keeps an assumed 10% reflected at 100 eV, more pessimistic than the model's 6%; (3) because signal and background were injected, the model's infrared output tests neither; (4) the positronium fraction is an input, and Geant4 decays ortho-positronium inside the film, while real positronium leaves the surface and decays centimetres away. A test run moved each decay to a point along the positronium's flight. If it still decays into three photons at the wall, the ratio changes by less than 2%. If the wall quenches it into two 511 keV photons (pick-off), the monitor reads f = 0.19 or 0.13 instead of 0.36. So the simulated monitor response is approximate, its zero point depends on the geometry (R = 0.028 for a bare film at 1 keV, 0.035 on silicon), and the monitor has to be calibrated in place.

Why the curve bends. \(R_\gamma\) is a ratio V/P. As f grows, V (the valley) goes up in a straight line and P (the peak) goes down in a straight line. A rising number divided by a falling one curves upward. The fix is simple: calibrate V and P separately (both are straight lines), or use the inverted formula above. The old slope, 0.87, came from three points at f ≤ 0.15 and is only right near zero.

S3. Infrared escape#

Transfer matrix with Ordal's Drude parameters for Al (plasma frequency 119 000 cm⁻¹, damping 660 cm⁻¹), angle-averaged. Key numbers: 0.104 at 3 nm, 0.0675 at 5 nm, 0.036 at 10 nm; thick-film floor 0.6–1%. Silicon backing changes 5 nm escape from 0.0675 to 0.0677.

S4. Positron stopping and positronium#

Makhov stopping profile and 150 nm diffusion length, as before. A sanity check reproduces the ~55% surface return at 3 keV (model: 58%). The positronium model is new. It is now the measured curve for clean Al(111), Eq. (S1): a temperature-independent 0.481 plus a thermally activated part with activation energy 0.344 eV that only matters above room temperature (Mills & Pfeiffer 1985). It gives 0.481 at 77, 150 and 200 K, 0.483 at 300 K and 0.67 at 450 K. An option uses the 24 L oxygen data instead (Mills et al. 1991): 0.63 at 77 K and 0.66 at 150 K, so the in-film fraction would differ by about 10% between the setpoints. Another holds the fraction fixed, to explore the 0.1–0.9 range. The old model (0.15 plus a 0.25 eV part normalized to 0.5 at 300 K) had no source and is kept only for comparison.

Two further notes. Positrons that stay behind may annihilate while stuck in a surface state, not inside the metal; the paper counts them as "in the film", and says this is an assumption. And an oxide on the film would push it toward the "thick oxide" case where positronium is suppressed, so the film should be made and kept in ultrahigh vacuum. None of these numbers comes from Geant4; the three-gamma rate measures them in place.

S5. Cavity temperature#

The wall/film ratio of in-band emission, assuming wall emissivity 0.01 and radius 4 cm: 6×10⁻⁸ (4 K), 0.011 (10 K), 0.64 (20 K) in the 77 K band. Hence 4–10 K, not 20 K.

S6. Beam heating and the substrate#

Requirement: the chopped change in thermal emission must be below 10% of the chopped signal. Tolerable temperature flicker: 1.4×10⁻⁸ K (77 K), 3.8×10⁻⁹ K (150 K). A bare 5 nm film would flicker by ~2×10⁻⁴ K; on 0.5 mm of high-resistivity silicon, ~1.1×10⁻⁹ K (margins 13 and 3.4; they were 21 and 5.6 with the old 15% positronium). The margin scales with the in-film fraction: at the top of the positronium range (f_film = 0.1) it would be 0.66 at 150 K, too small. The ratio is independent of \(R\), since heating and signal both scale with the beam.

S7. Noise model and the \(T^3\) law#

Supplement · S7, Eq. (S3)\(P_\mathrm{th} = \eta f_\mathrm{coll}\,2\mathcal{A}\varepsilon\,\frac{2\pi k_b^4T^4}{h^3c^2}\int_{x_0(1-b/2)}^{x_0(1+b/2)}\frac{x^3dx}{e^x-1} \propto T^4\)

Substituting \(x = h\nu/k_bT\) shows the band integral doesn't depend on \(T\) (the band moves with \(T\)), leaving \(T^4\). Then NEP ∝ \(T^{5/2}\), signal ∝ \(T\), time ∝ \(T^3\). The script checks each ratio:

S8. Optics#

Not designed in detail; η = 0.5 is assumed. Three requirements: strong out-of-band rejection (the film's total glow is far larger than its in-band glow), cold filters and light pipe, and a detector covering 92–179 µm plus the control band.

Questions a referee might ask#

Q1. How far should I trust the Geant4 numbers?

Honest answer: they were re-run on a newer Geant4 (11.4.2) and agree with the first runs within 2%. The two that matter are easy to sanity-check: the collected fraction 0.984 equals the geometric coverage 0.983, and the stopping depth only sets the film thickness, which has a factor-of-three margin. At 100 eV the transport is not reliable (half the positrons stop at exactly the same depth), so a dedicated low-energy implantation calculation would be better there. The positronium fraction is an input to Geant4, not a result.

Q2. Your heating margin at 150 K is only 3.4. Isn't that thin?

Honest answer: yes, and it got thinner when the positronium fraction was corrected (it was 5.6). It uses silicon's room-temperature conductivity, which is several times lower than its value at 77–150 K, and a steady-state (worst-case) response, so the real margin should be larger. But if the film turned out to emit 90% positronium, the margin would drop below one and better heat sinking would be needed. The control band monitors any residual heating directly.

Q3. Each annihilation makes two gamma photons. How could the first runs record 2.10 per annihilation?

Honest answer: because 2.10 is not a photon count. The detector code writes a row whenever a track starts a step in the shell, either by crossing into it or by being created inside it, and the count kept every row above 400 keV. The re-run follows each annihilation photon separately and splits the 2.107 into 1.934 rows from photons crossing in, 0.173 from tracks created inside the crystal above 400 keV, and 0.0002 from anything else. The created tracks are photoelectrons: when LaBr₃ absorbs a 511 keV photon it ejects an electron with nearly all of that energy, and the code counted it as a second entry. Photons do re-enter the shell (about 6% of those that enter come back a second time), but after scattering they are almost never above 400 keV, so re-entry adds almost nothing. The real efficiencies: at least one photon deposits more than 50 keV in 78% of annihilations, both in 28%.

An older note in the code archive explained the extra 0.10 as "two gammas plus the ortho-positronium three-gamma fraction". That cannot be right, for two reasons. First, the first runs contained no positronium decays (S2). Second, a three-photon decay makes the count go down: it replaces two 511 keV photons with three photons sharing 1022 keV, and those are mostly below 400 keV. The re-run confirms the direction: with 15% ortho-positronium the old count falls from 2.11 to 1.98, and the 10 Oct scan shows it falls in a straight line, 2.085 − 0.846 f (1.78 at the nominal f = 0.36). The main text never used the 2.10 figure, so no result changes.

Key takeaways#

  • Table S1 is the honest ledger: most inputs are assumptions or design choices.
  • Geant4 supplies geometry, transport and the gamma-shell response only, with stated limits; it was re-run with Geant4 11.4.2, which agrees with the first runs within 2%.
  • The \(T^3\) law, the 4–10 K wall and the silicon substrate are derived in S5–S7.
