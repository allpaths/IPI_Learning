How to read this page

Code cells with a blue edge are LaTeX source, copied from main.tex or references.bib (the line numbers are real). Cells with a green edge show what the PDF prints. If you only have five minutes, read §1, §2 and §7 (compiling), then keep the cheat sheet in §9 open while you edit.

1. LaTeX vs Word: why switch#

In Word you format the page directly: you see what you get. In LaTeX you write plain text with markup (\section{...}, \cite{...}, $...$), and a program, the compiler, lays out the page for you. You lose live preview. In return you get several things that matter for a physics paper:

TaskWordLaTeX (this paper)
Journal formatMatch fonts, headings, captions by hand.One line, \documentclass[aip,adv,...]{revtex4-2}, gives AIP Advances' own layout.
EquationsEquation editor; numbering by hand or by fields that break.Typeset to publication quality; numbered automatically.
Cross-references"Eq. (4)" typed by hand; goes stale when you add an equation.Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda} always prints the right number.
ReferencesNumbered by hand or by a plug-in; easy to get out of order.BibTeX numbers them in citation order and formats them in AIP style (§6).
Version controlBinary .docx; diffs are unreadable.Plain text; every change shows up line by line in Git.
SubmissionAIP accepts Word.AIP accepts LaTeX and provides the official REVTeX template; it is the normal route for physics papers.

The price: you will see error messages, and you need to compile to see the result. Overleaf (§7) makes both nearly painless.

2. The structure of main.tex#

Every LaTeX file has two parts. The preamble (everything before \begin{document}) chooses the document class and loads packages. The body (between \begin{document} and \end{document}) is the paper. Here is our preamble:

% manuscript/main.tex 12 \documentclass[aip,adv,preprint,amsmath,amssymb]{revtex4-2} 13 14 \usepackage{graphicx} % \includegraphics 15 \usepackage{dcolumn} % decimal-aligned columns (AIP template default) 16 \usepackage{bm} % bold math 17 \usepackage[separate-uncertainty=true,per-mode=symbol]{siunitx} % units: \SI{45.9}{\micro\metre} 18 \sisetup{range-phrase=--, range-units=single} 19 \usepackage[hidelinks]{hyperref} % clickable refs/DOIs; load last 20 % NOTE: with revtex4-2 + hyperref, nameref prints a benign warning 21 % "The definition of \label has changed" -- harmless; it 22 % appears even in a minimal revtex4-2 aip/adv file that loads hyperref. 23 ⋮ 25 \newcommand{\kb}{k_b} % Boltzmann constant, Vopson's notation 26 \newcommand{\eminus}{e^{-}} 27 \newcommand{\eplus}{e^{+}}
  • revtex4-2 is the class used by AIP and APS journals. aip selects AIP Publishing's style, adv selects AIP Advances in particular, and preprint gives the double-spaced single-column layout AIP asks for at submission. Swap preprint for reprint to preview the two-column journal look (and check length); swap back before submitting.
  • \usepackage{...} loads extra tools. siunitx typesets numbers with units; hyperref makes every reference clickable and should be loaded last.
  • \newcommand defines shortcuts. \kb prints Vopson's \(k_b\). Changing the notation everywhere is a one-line edit.
  • % starts a comment. Everything after it on the line is ignored. Lines starting with %% are notes for the author; they never appear in the PDF (they are omitted from the snippets on this page).

The body opens with the front matter: title, author, abstract. REVTeX collects these and prints them when it reaches \maketitle:

% manuscript/main.tex 35 36 \title[Detecting information energy photons from positron annihilation]% 37 {Design of an experiment to detect information energy photons 38 from electron--positron annihilation} 39 40 \author{Jeff Dillon} 41 \email[Author to whom correspondence should be addressed: ]{jeff@sumoverpaths.ai} ⋮ 48 \affiliation{Information Physics Institute, Gosport, Hampshire, United Kingdom} 49 50 \date{\today} 51 52 \begin{abstract} 53 The mass--energy--information equivalence principle proposes that a 54 stored bit of information has an equivalent mass. Vopson has suggested 55 a direct test: when an electron and a positron annihilate, the 56 information they carry is erased, and if the principle holds, its 57 energy should leave as two infrared photons besides the two 58 511~keV gamma photons. The predicted wavelength is inversely 59 proportional to the temperature of the annihilation site, about 60 179~\textmu m at 77~K and 92~\textmu m at 150~K for 1.509 bits per 61 particle. We turn that proposal into a design and estimate its 62 response. A chopped slow-positron beam is implanted at 63 100~eV into a 5~nm aluminum film held at 77~K and then at 150~K, 64 inside a cold cavity viewed by a far-infrared bolometer and surrounded 65 by gamma-ray detectors. The signature is a line that 66 moves between two filter bands when the film temperature changes. Only about 7\% of the infrared photons escape the reflective 67 metal film, and we assume that about 15\% of positrons leave the film as 68 positronium. The noise is set by the film's own thermal emission, so 69 colder operation is faster. The quantum electrodynamic soft-photon 70 background in the band is at least four orders of magnitude below 71 the predicted signal. A 5$\sigma$ detection then takes 72 about 30~min at 77~K and 4~h at 150~K at the slow-positron rate of 73 $4.5\times10^{7}$~s$^{-1}$ demonstrated at a reactor-based facility, but 74 years with a laboratory $^{22}$Na source. 75 \end{abstract} 76 77 \maketitle

The optional [...] after \email sets the footnote text, which is how AIP marks the corresponding author. The rest of the body is \section / \subsection commands and ordinary paragraphs. A blank line starts a new paragraph; single line breaks in the source are ignored, so you can wrap lines wherever you like.

% manuscript/main.tex 80 \section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro} ⋮ 138 \section{The prediction}\label{sec:prediction} ⋮ 140 \subsection{Information content of the electron and positron}\label{sec:info} ⋮ 168 \subsection{Erasure by annihilation}\label{sec:erasure} ⋮ 192 \subsection{Energy and wavelength of the information energy photons}\label{sec:wavelength} ⋮ 268 \subsection{Assumptions}\label{sec:assumptions} ⋮ 312 \section{Experimental protocol}\label{sec:protocol} ⋮ 382 \section{Experimental design}\label{sec:design} ⋮ 388 \subsection{Slow-positron source}\label{sec:source} ⋮ 421 \subsection{Target}\label{sec:target} ⋮ 458 \subsection{Gamma-ray detection}\label{sec:gamma} ⋮ 481 \subsection{Far-infrared detection}\label{sec:ir} ⋮ 517 \section{Expected response}\label{sec:response} ⋮ 519 \subsection{How the estimates are made}\label{sec:methods} ⋮ 558 \subsection{Signal and background spectra}\label{sec:background} ⋮ 617 \subsection{Losses in the target}\label{sec:losses} ⋮ 695 \subsection{Noise and time to a detection}\label{sec:sensitivity} ⋮ 790 \subsection{Systematic effects}\label{sec:systematics} ⋮ 809 \subsection{What a result would mean}\label{sec:meaning} ⋮ 833 \section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

REVTeX numbers sections with Roman numerals (I, II, …) and subsections with letters (A, B, …), as AIP does. You never type the numbers.

The end of the file holds the AIP back matter (a pointer to the Supplementary Material, Author Declarations, Data Availability) and one line that pulls in the reference list:

% manuscript/main.tex 885 \section*{Author Declarations} 886 \subsection*{Conflict of Interest} 887 The author has no conflicts to disclose. 888 889 \subsection*{Author Contributions} 890 \textbf{Jeff Dillon:} Conceptualization (lead); Methodology (lead); 891 Software (lead); Formal analysis (lead); Investigation (lead); 892 Visualization (lead); Writing -- original draft (lead); 893 Writing -- review \& editing (lead). 894 895 \section*{Data Availability} ⋮ 900 The data that support the findings of this study are available from 901 the corresponding author upon reasonable request. 902 903 \bibliography{references} 904 905 \end{document}

3. Maths: inline, display, numbered#

Inline maths: $...$

Anything between single dollar signs is set as maths inside the sentence:

% manuscript/main.tex 82 Landauer's principle states that erasing information costs energy: at 83 least $\kb T\ln 2$ per bit at temperature $T$, released as 84 heat.\cite{landauer1961} The mass--energy--information (M/E/I)
Landauer's principle states that erasing information costs energy: at least k_b T ln 2 per bit at temperature T, released as heat.¹

Note \ln (an upright "ln", not italic l·n) and the superscript citation number placed after the punctuation: that is AIP house style.

Numbered display equations

A displayed, numbered equation uses the equation environment. The \label gives it a name you can refer to later:

% manuscript/main.tex 181 Vopson's 2022 protocol [Eq.~(10) of Ref.~\onlinecite{vopson2022}] writes 182 the annihilation as 183 \begin{equation} 184 m_{\eminus}c^{2} + m_{\eplus}c^{2} 185 + I_{\eminus}\kb T\ln 2 + I_{\eplus}\kb T\ln 2 186 = \gamma + \gamma + \nu^{+} + \nu^{-} , 187 \label{eq:balance} 188 \end{equation} 189 where $\nu^{+}$ and $\nu^{-}$ are the two additional \emph{information 190 energy photons}.

Useful pieces: ^{...} superscript, _{...} subscript, \frac{a}{b} fraction, \nu ν, \gamma γ, \, a thin space, \mathrm{bit} an upright subscript (a label, not a variable):

% manuscript/main.tex 149 mass,\cite{vopson2019,vopson2022} 150 \begin{equation} 151 m_{\mathrm{bit}} = \frac{\kb T\ln 2}{c^{2}} . 152 \label{eq:mbit} 153 \end{equation}

The full stop or comma that ends the sentence goes inside the equation. For an unnumbered display, use \begin{equation*} or \[ ... \].

Referring to equations: \label and \eqref

Never type an equation number. Write \eqref{name} and LaTeX fills it in, with parentheses:

\SI{value}{unit} gives the correct upright units and a proper thin space ("45.9 µm"); \si{...} is a unit on its own; \SIrange writes "25–75 µm". Prefixes are words: \micro, \milli, \kilo, \tera. This avoids pasting a "µ" character, a common source of errors (§8).

4. Figures#

% manuscript/main.tex 256 \begin{figure} 257 \centering 258 \includegraphics[width=3.37in]{figures/fig_wavelength_vs_T} 259 \caption{Predicted wavelength of the information energy photons versus 260 target temperature, from Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda}, for 1, 1.509 and 261 3~bits per particle [after Fig.~2 of Ref.~\onlinecite{vopson2022}]. 262 Open circles mark the temperatures in Table~\ref{tab:prediction}. On 263 logarithmic axes the $1/T$ dependence appears as a straight line of 264 slope $-1$.} 265 \label{fig:lambda} 266 \end{figure}
  • figure is a float: LaTeX moves it to where it fits best (often the top of the next page). This is normal; AIP repositions figures in production anyway. Refer to it by label (Fig.~\ref{fig:lambda}), never as "the figure below".
  • \includegraphics is given the file name without an extension. pdfLaTeX picks up figures/fig_wavelength_vs_T.pdf, the vector version, so lines and lettering stay sharp at any zoom. The 600 dpi PNG next to it is for systems that need a raster file.
  • width=3.37in is the single-column width of an AIP journal page. The figure was drawn at exactly that size with 8–9 pt lettering, so it is not scaled and the text stays at least 8 pt, as AIP requires.
  • The \label goes after \caption; otherwise the reference picks up the wrong number.

5. Tables#

% manuscript/main.tex 230 \begin{table} 231 \caption{Predicted energy and wavelength of each information energy 232 photon, from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:energy} and \eqref{eq:lambda}, for 233 $I = 1.509$~bits.} 234 \label{tab:prediction} 235 \begin{ruledtabular} 236 \begin{tabular}{ 237 S[table-format=3.0] 238 S[table-format=2.3] 239 S[table-format=2.3] 240 S[table-format=4.1] 241 S[table-format=1.3]} 242 {$T$ (\si{\kelvin})} & 243 {$\kb T\ln 2$ per bit (\si{\milli\electronvolt})} & 244 {$h\nu$ (\si{\milli\electronvolt})} & 245 {$\lambda$ (\si{\micro\metre})} & 246 {$\nu$ (\si{\tera\hertz})} \\ 247 \colrule 248 4 & 0.239 & 0.361 & 3439 & 0.087 \\ 249 77 & 4.60 & 6.94 & 178.6 & 1.68 \\ 250 150 & 8.96 & 13.52 & 91.7 & 3.27 \\ 251 300 & 17.92 & 27.04 & 45.9 & 6.54 \\ 252 \end{tabular} 253 \end{ruledtabular} 254 \end{table}
  • Columns are separated by &; each row ends with \\.
  • ruledtabular is REVTeX's wrapper. It draws the double rules at top and bottom and stretches the table to the column width, as in AIP journals. \colrule draws the line under the header.
  • The column type S[table-format=4.1] (from siunitx) aligns numbers on the decimal point: up to 4 digits before the point and 1 after. That is why 3439 sits to the left of 178.6 in the PDF. Header cells are wrapped in {...} so siunitx treats them as text.
  • The caption goes above a table (AIP style); REVTeX prints "TABLE I." with a Roman numeral.

6. Citations with BibTeX#

The references live in a separate database, references.bib. Each entry has a key (here vopson2022) and fields. All 22 entries in this paper were checked against Crossref, including the DOI:

% manuscript/references.bib 51 @article{vopson2022, 52 author = {Vopson, Melvin M.}, 53 title = {Experimental protocol for testing the mass--energy--information equivalence principle}, 54 journal = {AIP Advances}, 55 volume = {12}, 56 number = {3}, 57 pages = {035311}, 58 year = {2022}, 59 doi = {10.1063/5.0087175} 60 }

In the text you cite by key. \cite gives AIP's superscript number; \onlinecite gives the bare number for use in a sentence ("Ref. 6"):

% manuscript/main.tex 143 dissipates at least $\kb T\ln 2$ of energy.\cite{landauer1961} This 144 bound has been tested with a colloidal particle in an optical 145 trap\cite{berut2012} and in a feedback trap,\cite{jun2014} and with 146 nanomagnetic memory bits.\cite{hong2016} The 147 mass--energy--information (M/E/I) equivalence principle goes one step 148 further. It proposes that a stored bit carries an equivalent 149 mass,\cite{vopson2019,vopson2022} ⋮ 161 Following Ref.~\onlinecite{vopson2022}, we assume that the positron ⋮ 181 Vopson's 2022 protocol [Eq.~(10) of Ref.~\onlinecite{vopson2022}] writes

At the end, \bibliography{references} asks BibTeX to print the list. REVTeX's aip option selects AIP's own bibliography style, aipnum4-2, automatically, so there is no \bibliographystyle line to get wrong.

Why the numbering can't go wrong

  1. Numbers are assigned on every compile, in order of first citation. aipnum4-2 is an "unsorted" style: the first key BibTeX meets in the text becomes [1], the next new one [2], and so on. In the paper that gives Landauer 1, Vopson 2019 2, Vopson 2021 3, Lairez 2024 4, Lairez 2025 5, Vopson 2022 6 (all cited in the Introduction), then Bérut 7, Jun 8, Hong 9 (first cited in Section II), and so on, exactly what the PDF shows.
  2. Adding or moving a citation renumbers everything, text and list together. Cite a new reference early in the text and every later number shifts, in the text and the list, with no hand edits. With hand-numbered references in Word, that is precisely where mistakes creep in.
  3. Only cited entries are printed. An entry in references.bib that is never \cited does not appear, and a reference you stop citing disappears from the list.
  4. Formatting is uniform. Author initials, bold volume, page range and year follow AIP's style for every entry, because the style file formats them, not you.
⚠ The one ruleA typo in a key (\cite{vopson2023}) does not stop the PDF. It prints ? or [vopson2023] and a "Citation undefined" warning. Search the PDF for "?" before you send it anywhere.

7. Compiling: three ways#

"Compiling" means running pdfLaTeX, then BibTeX (to build the reference list), then pdfLaTeX twice more (to settle all numbers and cross-references). You never need to do this by hand: Overleaf, latexmk and LaTeX Workshop all run the whole sequence for you.

A. Overleaf (recommended)

Overleaf is LaTeX in the browser: nothing to install, a full TeX Live with REVTeX already included, and a PDF preview next to the source. AIP's author instructions point authors to its AIP template there.

  1. Zip the manuscript/ folder (it must contain main.tex, references.bib and the figures/ folder).
  2. Go to overleaf.com, sign in (a free account is enough), and click New Project → Upload Project. Choose the zip.
  3. Overleaf opens the project. If it asks, set the main document: Menu → Main document → main.tex. While you are there, check Compiler → pdfLaTeX.
  4. Click Recompile. The PDF appears on the right in a few seconds.
  5. Click the log icon next to Recompile to see errors and warnings. Each one links to the line that caused it.
  6. Edit, then Recompile (or press Ctrl+S / ⌘+S). Double-click in the PDF to jump to the source line.
  7. To get files back: Menu → Download → Source (zip) or the PDF download button. Copy them back into your local manuscript/ folder so it stays the master copy.

B. Local install: TeX Live or MiKTeX, with latexmk

  • macOS: install MacTeX (the macOS build of TeX Live).
  • Linux: install TeX Live from your package manager. On Debian/Ubuntu, this paper compiled with texlive-latex-extra texlive-publishers texlive-science latexmk (REVTeX is in texlive-publishers).
  • Windows: install MiKTeX (installs missing packages on first use) or TeX Live. latexmk is a Perl script; with MiKTeX you may also need Strawberry Perl.

Then, in a terminal:

cd manuscript latexmk -pdf main.tex # runs pdflatex, bibtex, pdflatex as often as needed -> main.pdf latexmk -pvc -pdf main.tex # optional: watch mode, recompiles every time you save latexmk -c # delete .aux/.log/... build files (keeps main.pdf)

Without latexmk, the manual sequence is pdflatex main, bibtex main, pdflatex main, pdflatex main.

C. VS Code with LaTeX Workshop

  1. Install a TeX distribution as in B.
  2. In VS Code, open the Extensions panel and install LaTeX Workshop (by James Yu).
  3. File → Open Folder… and choose manuscript. Open main.tex.
  4. Save the file. LaTeX Workshop builds automatically with its default latexmk recipe. (Or use the TeX icon in the sidebar → Build LaTeX project.)
  5. Click View LaTeX PDF (the icon at the top right of the editor) to open the PDF in a tab. Ctrl+click in the PDF jumps to the source; errors appear in the Problems panel.

8. Common errors and what they mean#

LaTeX errors look alarming but are usually one-character fixes. Read the first error only; later ones are often knock-on effects. The log gives the line number as l.134.

MessageUsual causeFix
Undefined control sequenceA typo in a command (\eqrf) or a command from a package that isn't loaded.Correct the spelling or add the \usepackage.
Missing $ insertedMaths outside $...$, typically _ or ^ in plain text (e.g. typing m_e in a sentence).Wrap in $...$, or escape the character as \_.
Strange output or Misplaced alignment tab character &One of the special characters # $ % & _ { } ~ ^ \ typed as text. A bare % silently comments out the rest of the line.Escape: \%, \&, \#, \_.
Citation `xyz' undefined; ? in the PDFKey misspelt, entry missing from references.bib, or BibTeX not run yet.Check the key; compile again with latexmk / Recompile.

File `figures/...' not foundWrong path or name; on Overleaf the figures/ folder wasn't uploaded.Paths are relative to main.tex and case-sensitive.
Unicode character µ (U+00B5) not set up for use with LaTeXA symbol pasted from Word or a web page.Use \si{\micro\metre}, $\mu$, -- for an en dash, ``...'' for quotes.
Runaway argument / File ended while scanningA missing closing }.Look just above the reported line for the unmatched brace.
Overfull \hbox (warning)A line that sticks into the margin, often a long URL or an unbreakable formula.Rephrase or allow a break (e.g. \allowbreak after an underscore in a file name); the current files have none.
